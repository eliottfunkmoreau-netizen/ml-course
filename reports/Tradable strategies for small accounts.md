# No published strategy clears the bar

**None of the 30 papers reviewed shows a strategy that clears the Hard to Fool and Quant-Arena bar, and none tests a long-only, few-position account at a USD 0.35 minimum commission.** Only seven report any net-of-cost figure, and the most rigorous recent net evaluation, Gencay (2026), rejects every strategy its LLM agents discovered. The ideas most likely to yield something tradable are slow and low in order count: value-plus-profitability built from EDGAR, the turn of the month on one ETF, opportunistic insider purchases, and year-over-year filing similarity. They are candidates for testing, not established edges, and each has a specific reason to fail. The price-only cross-sectional family you already rejected is where the literature also shows the worst cost and decay record, so these papers give no reason to reopen it. The strict bar also misjudges the shortlisted rule types in opposite directions, because a long-biased portfolio passes an absolute bootstrap on market drift alone and a cash-heavy rule cannot beat buy-and-hold in a bull market. The single best first test is turn-of-month on SPY for January 2006 onward, the window after the evidence read ends, using data you already hold. Whether an EU-resident retail account can buy SPY at all is unverified and decides whether a pass could ever go live. This report is a research synthesis and is not personalized investment advice.

## Slow fundamentals and calendars beat your rejected price signals on the evidence, without clearing it

Your 16 rejections match the literature. Short-run reversal earns 0.37% per month gross and loses 1.28% net, while idiosyncratic volatility nets 0.11% ([Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)), and decay is worst for signals built only from price and trading data ([McLean and Pontiff](https://counterpointfunds.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/PredictabilityMcleanPontiff.pdf)). Momentum is the partial exception: it nets 0.68% per month in long-short deciles at 34.52% monthly turnover, which is neither your long-only large-cap design nor your cost structure. **None of the 30 papers tests FINRA short volume or off-exchange share**, so they offer no reason to reopen those rejections and no independent support for them.

The cost picture inverts the institutional one. Market impact is irrelevant at this size, and order count drives cost, so a rule's viability depends on orders per year, not percentage turnover. Only two strategies above 50% monthly turnover keep significant net spreads even at institutional costs ([Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)). Daily overnight holding on one ETF costs USD 176.40 a year, or 7.17% of the account, and 14.3% at doubled cost, which rules it out by arithmetic. Headline-sentiment signals fail for the same reason: Lopez-Lira and Tang's edge is gone at 20 bps round trip and absorbed in about two days ([Lopez-Lira and Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)). Filing-based signals are the only text family whose horizon can amortize USD 0.70 ([Lazy Prices](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084)).

The expected edge is small after a realistic haircut. Applying McLean and Pontiff's 58% decay to value-profitability's 0.82% monthly gross long-short spread gives about 4.1% a year. Assuming the long leg captures half, which no paper read establishes, the active return is about 2.1% a year. With an assumed 10% tracking error for 10 names, 14 annual formations give t of about 0.8. A real edge of that size would fail a benchmark-relative bootstrap most of the time, so a failure would not disprove it and a pass would surprise. Gencay's arithmetic makes the same point: a true Sharpe of 0.6 needs about eleven years to reach t of 2 ([Gencay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)).

### Ten conflicts remain unresolved

The replication debate is largely about hurdles. Hou, Xue and Zhang report 65% failure on microcap-limited samples, whereas Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen report 84% replication, including in mega and large caps ([Hou, Xue, Zhang](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf); [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)). Neither settles net profitability, which is the point that matters here and which Chen and Zimmermann explicitly leave open ([Chen and Zimmermann](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2021-037pap.pdf)).

Gross profitability is contested on construction. It nets 0.37% per month [2.74] using current assets, but gross profit over lagged assets earns only 0.16% (t=1.04), and Fama-French operating profit to book equity is listed as insignificant ([Hou, Xue, Zhang](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf)). Turn-of-month is disputed too: McConnell and Xu directly disagree with Maberly and Waggoner's finding that it vanished in S&P 500 futures after 1990, and no paper read tests ETFs after 2005 ([McConnell and Xu](https://apps.it.purdue.edu/sites/Home/DirectoryApi/Files/fed4cdba-2490-4cc5-893d-9f0d5efb626f/Download)). A secondary, paywalled report of 0.0% net CAGR for a turn-of-month close-to-open SPY variant is a different rule with eight round trips a month, so it does not resolve this ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/guides/intraday-overnight/)).

Trend following has a strong record in Moskowitz and Hurst, yet Huang et al. find it statistically weak and barely distinct from a rule that needs no predictability ([Huang et al.](https://down.aefweb.net/WorkingPapers/w717.pdf)). Volatility scaling shows market alpha of 4.86% for Moreira and Muir and a real-time Sharpe below the unmanaged market for Cederburg et al. ([Moreira and Muir](https://amoreira2.github.io/alan-moreira.github.io/VolPortfolios_published.pdf); [Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). PEAD is declared dead by Martineau on analyst surprises, but a different, return-based drift factor is reported alive in secondary material only ([Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf); [UCLA brief](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)). The earnings premium is either gone since 2004 or moved to 8-K periods, an additional 32 bps by Heitz et al.'s account ([Heitz et al.](https://www.iimb.ac.in/ARC2020/Papers/The_Disappearing_Earnings_Announcement_Premium.pdf)).

Insider evidence splits on size: Cohen et al. say predictability is not limited to smaller firms, while only 22.0% of Jeng et al.'s purchase portfolio by value sits in the largest third of stocks ([Cohen et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16454/w16454.pdf); [Jeng et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w6913/w6913.pdf)). LLM evidence divides by input and period: Chen and Pu's positive live run is nine months long and internally inconsistent, whereas Gencay's null excluded text features, so neither can be read as a verdict on filing text ([Chen and Pu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11958); [Gencay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). The last conflict sits in our own specifications. The research notes define the bootstrap once on absolute weekly returns and once net of benchmark, a difference that changes the outcome and is settled in the next section.

## Four of the six bar criteria need pre-registered definitions

The bar was built to catch fooled backtests, and it does so best for fully invested or market-neutral strategies judged against a like-for-like benchmark. A long-biased book collects equity drift on every absolute test: a random-weights null earned +23% over nine evaluation years in Gencay's calibration ([Gencay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). Choi's lowest-ranked quintile still earned 1.17% per month in raw excess return ([Choi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03195)). Huang's profits sit in the long leg, which suggests much of a long-only trend result is asset drift ([Huang et al.](https://down.aefweb.net/WorkingPapers/w717.pdf)). Cash-heavy rules face the opposite problem. A rule invested 4 of about 21 days can only beat buy-and-hold in samples where the other days earn about zero.

| Beats benchmark | Cash-heavy rules cannot beat buy-and-hold in a bull market; trend rules trade return for drawdown | H1: beat 0.19 times the SPY daily return, the exposure-matched synthetic, and the window-versus-other-days difference must be positive. H2-H4: equal-weight eligible universe. H5: same five ETFs, equal-weight |
| Opposite side fails | Bottom-ranked long portfolios and "long below trend" rules still earn drift and pass absolute tests | Opposite side must fail the active test, and long-minus-opposite must have a positive bootstrap lower bound |

Run both forms and report both. Only the strict form supports the claim "clears the Hard to Fool and Quant-Arena bar", and a rule passing only the adapted form should be labelled exactly that. Multiple testing needs one more choice: gate on Bonferroni across these five hypotheses, and report a deflated Sharpe with N=21, counting your 16 rejections. Deflation does not detect leakage, since a planted look-ahead oracle passes it outright ([Gencay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). A further limit applies to every historical run. These rules were proposed by a model with training data through about June 2026 and draw on published results, so a backtest is a replication and only post-registration forward results are clean evidence.

## Five hypotheses, led by the turn of the month

Ranking reflects evidence for the effect, fit with your data, order count and how decisively the test can resolve. Parameters not attributed to a paper are design choices to freeze before running. Half-spreads are unsourced assumptions and should be replaced with measured IBKR Level 1 quotes.

### H1. Turn-of-month on SPY

**Rule.** On the NYSE calendar, Day -1 is the month's last trading day and Day +3 is the next month's third trading day ([McConnell and Xu](https://apps.it.purdue.edu/sites/Home/DirectoryApi/Files/fed4cdba-2490-4cc5-893d-9f0d5efb626f/Download)). Buy SPY at the close of Day -2, sell at the close of Day +3, and hold cash at 0% otherwise. This captures the close-to-close returns of Days -1, +1, +2 and +3, on dividend-adjusted closes. Primary window is January 2006 to latest, which is after the evidence read; full SPY history is reported separately, and RSP is a disclosed variant only. Costs are USD 0.35 per order plus an assumed 1 bp half-spread. The opposite side is long from the Day +3 close to the next Day -2 close. **Data:** SPY daily OHLCV, NYSE calendar. **Turnover:** 24 orders a year, USD 8.40 or 0.34% of the account, plus about 0.24% spread, doubling to about 1.16%, in the market about 19% of days. **Main reason it could fail:** the premium decayed after 2005, which no source read can rule out. The strict benchmark test is likely to fail regardless.

### H2. Value-profitability, long-only

**Rule.** Universe is US-listed common stock on NYSE, NASDAQ or NYSE American, excluding SIC 6000-6999, ETFs and ADRs, top 1,000 by market cap, price at least USD 5, 60-day median dollar volume at least USD 20 million. On the last NYSE trading day of June from 2012, use first-reported 10-K values with `filed` before that date, for fiscal years ending in the prior calendar year. GP is `GrossProfit`, else revenue minus cost of revenue. GPA is GP over `Assets`. BM is `StockholdersEquity` over last-December market cap, excluding non-positive book equity. Score is rank(BM) plus rank(GPA) ([Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)). Hold 10 names equal-weight, starting with the top 10. Each June, sell only holdings outside the top 20% of score and fill slots with the highest-score unheld names in the top 10%, following the paper's buy/hold band. Fill at the next session's open. GP/A alone and GP over lagged assets are ledgered disclosures and are not pass criteria. The opposite side is the 10 lowest-score names. **Data:** EDGAR `companyfacts` (never `frames`, which embeds later revisions, per the [SEC API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)), OHLCV including delisted names. **Turnover:** at most 20 orders or USD 7.00, 0.28% a year. At NMV's 2.94% monthly rate, about 35% a year, or about 7 orders and USD 2.45. **Main reason it could fail:** about 14 annual formations of 10 names cannot separate a 2% active return from noise, and the published result is a long-short decile whose long leg was never shown.

### H3. Opportunistic insider purchases

**Rule.** Event is an original Form 4 with a non-derivative code-P acquisition by an officer or director of at least USD 10,000, excluding plan trades flagged 10b5-1. Each January 1, classify insiders using only filings accepted before that date. Routine means a trade in the same calendar month in each of at least three preceding years; opportunistic means at least three years of history and not routine ([Cohen et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16454/w16454.pdf)). Universe at the prior close is common stock, price at least USD 5, market cap above USD 500 million and 60-day median dollar volume above USD 5 million. Enter at the open of the first NYSE session strictly after the calendar date of the EDGAR acceptance time. Hold five equal slots first-come, ties broken by accession number, cash at 0% when unfilled. Exit at the open after 126 trading days; 21 and 63 days are ledgered variants. Routine buys and opportunistic sells used as long signals are the opposite side. **Data:** Form 4 XML, `acceptanceDateTime`, OHLCV including delisted names. **Turnover:** at most about 20 orders, USD 7.00 or 0.28% a year at full slots. **Main reason it could fail:** evidence read ends in 2007, and the public filing may arrive after most of the return, as an unverified blog lead claims. A market-cap floor also cuts where Jeng et al. found most of the portfolio's weight.

### H4. Mechanical filing similarity

**Rule.** Universe is the top 1,000 by point-in-time market cap, price at least USD 10, 63-day median dollar volume at least USD 20 million. Formation is the last trading day of March, June, September and December. Eligible filings are original 10-K or 10-Q forms accepted that quarter by 16:00 ET on the prior session, with a same-form, same-fiscal-period filing from the same CIK accepted 330 to 400 days earlier. Strip HTML, tables and exhibits, lowercase, keep alphabetic tokens, and compute cosine similarity of term-frequency vectors ([Lazy Prices](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084)). Hold the top 5 by similarity, ties to the larger cap, equal-weight, filled at the next open, replacing only names that leave the top 5. The opposite side is the bottom 5, with a seeded random-5 control. Record N=4 in the ledger, since cosine was picked from four measures. **Data:** EDGAR filing text and timestamps, OHLCV including delisted names. **Turnover:** at most 40 orders, USD 14.00 or 0.57% a year. **Main reason it could fail:** a thin 0.23% monthly long-leg alpha from 1995-2014, with no post-2014 evidence read, swamped by five-name noise.

### H5. Faber five-sleeve trend

**Rule.** Hold 20% each in SPY, EFA, IEF, GSG and VNQ, chosen to track Faber's indices, subject to an inception-date check. At the last NYSE trading day of each month, hold a sleeve if its dividend-adjusted close exceeds the mean of its last 10 month-end closes, otherwise hold cash at 0% ([Faber](https://mebfaber.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/SSRN-id962461.pdf)). Fill at the next open, trading only on signal changes or when a weight drifts more than 25% from target. BIL as the cash sleeve is a disclosed variant. The benchmark is the same five ETFs equal-weight, rebalanced annually. The opposite side holds each ETF only when below its SMA. **Data:** ETF daily OHLCV; history likely starts around 2007-2009. **Turnover:** 3-4 round trips, USD 2.10-2.80 or 9-11 bps a year. Holding cash through BIL doubles orders to 12-16 and costs about 17-23 bps. **Main reason it could fail:** the documented benefit is lower drawdown rather than return, and Faber's post-2005 outperformance is dominated by avoiding 2008. A sample starting after that crash has little to show.

The LLM arm stays off the shortlist because it cannot be evaluated on this bar within a useful period. Running Choi's moving-targets method on MD&A as a forward-only, hash-logged experiment is cheap, but any LLM-scored backtest before about June 2026 is contaminated by model memory, and the port from transcripts to filings has no published validation ([Choi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03195)). Barton's null-arm and reconstructed-fill requirements should govern it if run ([Barton et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663)).

### The single best first test is H1 on 2006 onward

H1 goes first because it needs only data you hold, adds one trial to the ledger, and has about 250 monthly cycles against about 14 formations for H2. It can also discriminate. Scaling McConnell and Xu's difference statistic of t=3.78 to about 250 cycles gives about 4.0 with no decay and about 1.7 with a 58% haircut, against a Bonferroni threshold of about 2.58. This scaling assumes similar volatility and borrows a decay rate estimated on cross-sectional anomalies. Freeze the benchmark definition first, run the opposite side in the same pass, and report both forms of the bar. If H1 fails the adapted form, drop it and move to H2 without tuning the window. If it passes only the adapted form, resolve the instrument blocker below before anything else, and then start a forward log, treating any two-year result as likely inconclusive at a moderate edge.

## Unverified blockers could stop live use even after a pass

**Access to US-domiciled ETFs is the largest open question.** EU retail clients are commonly restricted by PRIIPs/KID rules, which would force UCITS substitutes with shorter or different histories, but the research never verified this for your account. H1 and H5 depend on it directly. A UCITS listing would also trade on a different venue and hours and may not carry the USD 0.35 minimum, so a SPY backtest would not automatically transfer. From general background that was not checked in the notes, ordinary shares are not packaged products, so H2, H3 and H4 should not face this restriction; confirm it with the broker. If ETF access fails, H2 becomes the first candidate for live use.

Several cost inputs also remain unverified: IBKR fractional-share eligibility, which matters because a USD 246-492 position may be less than one share of some names; the FX cost of a EUR-base account buying USD instruments, which the notes warn could exceed commission; and per-share IBKR tiers above the minimum. Market-on-close and market-on-open availability and slippage, ETF spreads and whether idle cash earns anything were not sourced. Tax treatment, including US dividend withholding and EU reporting, was not researched at all.

Data blockers can invalidate backtests silently. Free price sources often drop delisted tickers, which biases H2, H3 and H4 upward, and the availability of delisted history for your feed was not established. EDGAR's phase-in dates came from a [search-result summary](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2009/sec-posts-mandatory-xbrl-release), XBRL tag coverage and missing cost-of-revenue tags were not measured, and the Form 4 XML start date and 10b5-1 field were not verified. The `acceptanceDateTime` example carries a `Z` suffix that should not be trusted as UTC until checked against filing index pages ([data.sec.gov sample](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0000320193.json)). ETF inception dates are from memory.

## Conclusion

The search changes what a good answer looks like here. Commission dominates at USD 2,460, so the viable set is defined by orders per year, and that points to a handful of slow rules that the literature has barely tested in long-only form. The larger lesson concerns the gate: a bar built to reject fooled backtests can pass a long-biased portfolio on drift and fail a real cash-heavy edge on definition, so the benchmark and the active-versus-absolute choice decide more outcomes than the signal does.

The realistic outcome is that most or all of these five fail, and that a real low-single-digit active edge in stocks would remain statistically indistinguishable from noise for a decade or more. A failed shortlist would still be a useful result, because the honest fallback for an account this size is to treat low-cost passive exposure as the benchmark to beat and require forward, registered evidence before believing anything does.
